project · 01

Substrate stiffness as a tuning knob for therapeutic tolerance.

Non-small-cell lung cancer cells respond to EGFR inhibitors very differently depending on the mechanical stiffness of the tissue around them. Soft environments — like healthy lung — kill the cells. Stiff environments — like tumor stroma — let some cells survive and keep dividing. The question was why.

I built a stochastic, mechanistic model of the cell-cycle decision in PySB, parameterized it with in-vitro measurements of mitotic time, quiescence, and death, and ran it across thousands of Gillespie trajectories per condition. The averages didn't tell the story; the distributions did. On stiff substrates, the cell population had measurably more access to a fast-dividing attractor — one the drug couldn't quite close off. 

# sketch: simulating one stiffness condition
from pysb.simulator import StochKitSimulator
sim = StochKitSimulator(model, tspan=tspan)
results = sim.run(
    initials=initials_from_stiffness(kPa=12.0),
    n_runs=2000,
)
trajectories = results.dataframe

Manuscript in preparation. Talks given at SBHD 2022 and Cancer Biology Science Hour 2023.

project · 02

Co-expression networks on melanoma RNA-seq.

A collaboration with the Richmond lab on CXCR2-driven melanoma. Bulk RNA-seq from tumors with and without functional CXCR2 — and the question of which gene programs sit downstream of the receptor.

I ran weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) over the full dataset, identified modules that tracked with tumor-growth phenotypes, and ran GO enrichment to make the modules legible. The pieces made it into Yang et al., Mol Cancer, 2023. 

# module detection, abridged
library(WGCNA)
sft <- pickSoftThreshold(expr, powerVector = 1:20)
net <- blockwiseModules(
  expr,
  power      = sft$powerEstimate,
  TOMType    = "signed",
  minModuleSize = 30,
  mergeCutHeight = 0.25
)

The interesting bit wasn't any single module — it was watching modules rearrange when CXCR2 was knocked out. A reminder that you're not really looking at genes; you're looking at programs.

project · 03

Arrestin recruitment, visualized.

Long before the modeling work, I spent two years in the Skiniotis lab collecting electron microscopy images of protein assemblies. The headline paper was a structure of a GPCR–arrestin complex that helped settle a long-standing question about how arrestin gets to the receptor — published in Nature in 2014.

Quieter contributions on the side: visualizing factor VIII bound to von Willebrand factor (with the Fuller and Ginsburg labs), and the architecture of VPS13 — a then-mysterious lipid-transport protein.

project · 04

An interactive two-state switch.

Built as a teaching aid: a tiny in-browser stochastic simulator. Two states, forward and reverse rates, a population of independent cells. Move the sliders; watch averages lie. Implemented in vanilla JS so it just works.
