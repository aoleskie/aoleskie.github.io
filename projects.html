research · 01

Substrate stiffness as a tuning knob for therapeutic tolerance.

Non-small-cell lung cancer cells respond to EGFR inhibitors very differently depending on the mechanical stiffness of the substrate around them. Soft environments — like healthy lung — sensitize the cells. Stiff environments — like tumor stroma — let some cells survive and keep dividing. The question was why.

I built a stochastic, mechanistic model of the cell fate decision in PySB, parameterized it with in-vitro measurements of mitotic time, quiescence, and death, and ran it across thousands of Gillespie trajectories per condition. ODEs failed to capture the full behavior of the system; the cell population had measurably more access to a fast-dividing attractor. 

# sketch: simulating one stiffness condition
from pysb.simulator import StochKitSimulator
sim = StochKitSimulator(model, tspan=tspan)
results = sim.run(
    initials=initials_from_stiffness(kPa=12.0),
    n_runs=2000,
)
trajectories = results.dataframe

Manuscript in preparation. Talks given at SBHD 2022 and Cancer Biology Science Hour 2023.

research · 02

Co-expression networks on melanoma RNA-seq.

A collaboration with the Richmond and Quaranta labs studying CXCR2-driven melanoma. Bulk RNA-seq from Braf/Pten tumors with and without Cxcr2 knockout — the question was which transcriptional programs sit downstream of the receptor. My contribution was the WGCNA portion of the analysis: building gene co-expression modules from the tumor RNA-seq, ranking transcription factors by their centrality to each module, and testing which modules were significantly different between wild-type and knockout. The analysis served as orthogonal support for a TFCP2L1 finding the rest of the team had identified through differential expression — placing TFCP2L1 at the center of a skin-development module that was upregulated in Cxcr2-null tumors. Published as part of Yang et al., Mol Cancer, 2023.

data · 01

Biotech funding patterns.

A side project tracking how money moves through biotech: NIH grant allocations by area, venture-capital flow into therapeutic categories, and IPO activity by year and subsector. Mostly an exercise in pulling public data from places that don't make it easy — NIH RePORTER, SEC EDGAR, Crunchbase exports — into a shape where year-over-year shifts are actually visible.

Writeup forthcoming. The interesting questions: which therapeutic areas are persistently overfunded relative to disease burden, and where the gap between academic interest and venture interest is widest.

data · 02

Board composition in public biotech.

Who sits on the boards of public biotech companies, and how often the same names show up across multiple boards. Pulling director rosters from SEC proxy filings (DEF 14A), building a graph where companies are nodes connected by shared directors, and looking at the structure.

Writeup forthcoming. The questions worth asking: how concentrated is the director pool, what fraction of new biotech boards include someone already on three or more, and whether interlock patterns predict anything about company outcomes.

tools · 01

A career scraper.

Built for myself during the job search. A small Python pipeline that scrapes the careers pages of a curated set of companies (mostly biotech, some data-science), normalizes the postings into a common schema, deduplicates them, and flags what's new since the last run. Output is a single CSV I can sort and filter.

The hard part wasn't scraping — it was that every company's careers page is built differently. About a third use Greenhouse, a third use Lever, and the rest each have their own bespoke setup. So the architecture is a base scraper class with per-platform subclasses, and one-off subclasses for the holdouts.

tools · 02

Hearts in the browser.

A browser implementation of the card game Hearts. Three AI opponents, rules-correct scoring (queen of spades = 13, hearts = 1, shoot the moon = 26 to everyone else), and pass-three-cards at the start of every hand except every fourth. Built mostly to teach myself game-state management in plain JavaScript without a framework.

The longer-term plan is to extend the same engine to Star Realms — a deckbuilder with a richer action space and a more interesting target for an AI opponent. Hearts is the warmup.
